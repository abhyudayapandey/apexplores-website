In late 2020, working from home had quietly become living at work, so a couple of friends and I picked the North Bengal hills — Mirik, Darjeeling, and Kalimpong — as somewhere remote, peaceful, and just about workable for a two-week WFH stretch. You don't need two weeks to get the best of this route; here's the shorter, non-pandemic version of that trip.

## North Bengal at a glance

- **Best time to visit:** October to February — clear mountain views, comfortable temperatures, and (outside of major holidays) manageable crowds.
- **How long you need:** 5-7 days covers Mirik, Darjeeling, and Kalimpong properly without rushing between them.
- **Budget:** Roughly ₹2,500-3,500 per day, covering stay, food, and local transport.
- **Getting around:** Hire a car for the full loop — the roads between towns are scenic but winding, and having your own vehicle (or driver) makes stopping at viewpoints painless.
- **One important caveat:** if Singalila National Park (Sandakphu) is on your list, know that it's a 2-day affair requiring either the park's own jeep or a guide traveling with you — not a same-day add-on. We skipped it for exactly this reason; plan ahead if you want to include it.

## Mirik: the easy starting point

Mirik is small, calm, and built around its lake — a short walk from most hotels, with a jungle trail on one side. It's a good first stop to adjust before heading further into the hills. The Mirik monastery is worth a look for the architecture alone, and on a clear day you can spot the Sleeping Buddha mountain range from town.

## Darjeeling: the main event

This is where you'll want most of your time. Beyond the well-known sights — the Peace Pagoda, Batasia Loop, the Himalayan Mountaineering Institute — the town itself, its markets, and the sunset views (Glenary's has a genuinely great one, even if the food itself is a little overrated) are worth wandering without a fixed plan.

A few places worth eating at: **Biryani Baar** for reliably good biryani, **Subba Restaurant** near Hotel Mahakal for momos, and **Chopstix** for a small, cozy sit-down meal. One warning: we heard of stomach issues after eating at Hotel Mahakal — worth being cautious there.

## Kalimpong: the quieter, better half

If you only do one "extra" stop beyond Darjeeling, make it Kalimpong. It's smaller, calmer, and — in our experience — more likeable than Darjeeling itself. Delo viewpoint is worth the visit (we caught a local football match there, at 5,600ft, which was its own kind of memorable), and the Bidhyang river valley is a genuinely peaceful spot to spend an afternoon, though getting there means asking locals for directions rather than trusting your map.

Try the local food here specifically — **Phambi** (a lentil-jelly soup) and **Laphing** (a noodle dish that looks like spring rolls) are both worth the confusion of ordering them for the first time.

## Practical tips

- **Never trust Maps in the mountains.** This isn't a throwaway line — more than once, following the suggested route led us onto barely-passable roads that weren't actually shorter, while the real, better-maintained road wasn't shown on Maps at all. When in doubt, ask a local.
- **Some places mentioned above are from a 2020 visit** — the recommendations still stand as an honest account, but double-check current status, especially for smaller family-run spots.
- **Budget a proper buffer day** for weather — the hills can fog in unpredictably, and a planned viewpoint visit isn't worth doing in zero visibility.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Darjeeling or Kalimpong better for a shorter trip?**
If you only have a few days, prioritize Darjeeling for the range of things to see. If you have the extra day or two, Kalimpong is the better, quieter experience and worth it precisely because it's less visited.

**Do I need a permit for this region?**
Not for Mirik, Darjeeling, or Kalimpong themselves. Singalila National Park (for Sandakphu) is the exception — that requires a permit and either a park jeep or your own guide, and is realistically a 2-day commitment on its own.

**Is this route doable without a car?**
It's possible with local buses and shared jeeps between towns, but a hired car makes the winding mountain roads and viewpoint stops far less of a headache — worth the cost if you can manage it.

**What's the best season to visit North Bengal's hill towns?**
October to February for clear views and comfortable weather. Avoid monsoon months if visibility and road conditions matter to your itinerary.
