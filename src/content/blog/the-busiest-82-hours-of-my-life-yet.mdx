Bir Billing is Asia's highest paragliding site, and it's easy to plan a trip around the flight alone. What actually makes it worth the trip is the whole sequence — the hike up from Bir to the Billing launch point, a night camping near the top, and then the flight down into the valley the next morning.

I did the whole thing on 4 days' notice, having never heard of the place until a random Monday scroll turned up "2nd highest paragliding site in the world, hosted a World Cup." That was enough.

## Bir Billing at a glance

- **Best time to visit:** March to June, for the warm winds that keep gliders flying well. October–November is the other solid window most operators recommend, though monsoon (July–September) shuts paragliding down entirely.
- **How long you need:** 3 days is enough — one to arrive and hike up, one for camping and the flight, one buffer/travel day. We did it in a tighter, messier 82-hour version; don't recommend that part.
- **Paragliding cost:** Current tandem rates run roughly ₹2,500–5,500 depending on flight duration and operator, with a standard 15-20 minute flight typically around ₹3,000. Prices shift a bit in peak season.
- **Booking:** You can book directly with an operator once you're in Bir, or arrange it in advance through a tour operator before you go — both work, direct booking gives you more flexibility on timing.
- **Weather matters more than your schedule:** avoid cloudy or rainy conditions — if the weather's not right, the flight gets pushed, so build in a spare day if you can.

## The hike up

We rented bicycles first — no, went straight into the hike from Bir to Billing, roughly 14km by road or a steeper, shorter route on foot. We chose the hike, and it took about 4.5-5 hours with a lunch break.

Along the way we passed shepherds resting with their flocks, and you know you're close once you start spotting paragliders overhead. We camped that night near the top — good food, a bonfire, singing, the works.

## The flight

The next morning brought cloud cover, which is bad news for paragliding — you need warmer air to stay up. We got ready and had breakfast anyway, not knowing if the flight was happening, until a pickup truck arrived with our instructors shouting to move fast. We abandoned breakfast, grabbed our bags, and ran down to the launch point.

The flight itself was worth every bit of the scramble — genuinely surreal, and if you're up for it, ask your instructor about doing a few stunts mid-air, it adds a lot.

## After landing

Once you're back in Bir, there's a good half-day left in most itineraries. We rented bicycles for a ride to the Palpung Sherabling Monastery — a 6km climb on paper that turned into 10km after we missed a turn and cut through a valley instead of backtracking, which turned out to be the better mistake, since it took us past a wild rubber-collection setup we wouldn't have otherwise seen.

## Practical tips

- **Rest before the trek, not just the flight.** The hike itself is the demanding part — 4.5-5 hours with real elevation gain — so treat the night before like you're prepping for a hike, not a party.
- **Weather has the final say.** If it's cloudy or rainy, the flight moves or gets cancelled. Don't plan a trip with zero buffer day.
- **Confirm what's included** when booking — insurance, entry fee, and the jeep transfer from Bir to the Billing launch point aren't always bundled into the quoted price.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Bir Billing paragliding safe for beginners?**
Yes — it's a tandem flight with a certified pilot, and no prior experience is needed. The main variable isn't skill, it's weather; a good operator will delay rather than fly in poor conditions.

**How much does paragliding at Bir Billing actually cost?**
Expect roughly ₹2,500-5,500 depending on flight length and operator, with a standard 15-20 minute tandem flight around ₹3,000 in most current listings. Longer cross-country-style flights run considerably higher.

**What's the best season to visit Bir Billing?**
March to June for stable, warm winds; October-November is the other strong window. Paragliding shuts down during monsoon (July-September).

**Do I need to book paragliding in advance?**
Not necessarily — you can book directly with a registered operator once you're in Bir. Booking ahead through a tour operator is also an option if you want it settled before you travel.
